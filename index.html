Topic 2.2: Modulation — Making a Sound Move

Modulation means one signal changes another signal over time[cite: 1]. A synth connects a modulation source (the control that moves) to a target (the setting that changes)[cite: 1]. Select a target below to inspect how a Low Frequency Oscillator (LFO) sculpts different acoustic effects[cite: 1].

Listening to: Vibrato (pitch wobbles 5 to 6 times per second).

Vibrato: The Wobble That Makes a Note Sing

A violinist rocks a finger back and forth on the string[cite: 1]. The pitch rises and falls slightly, which makes the note warm[cite: 1]. On a synth, an LFO sent to pitch does the same[cite: 1]. Set the speed to about 5 to 6 Hz (cycles per second) and the depth to a small amount[cite: 1].

Analogy: a singer’s vibrato. A singer’s voice wobbles up and down a little, several times per second[cite: 1]. An LFO does that steady wobble[cite: 1].
Control flow versus sound flow: The sound flow is the audio path: oscillator, then filter, then amplifier[cite: 1]. Control flow is the set of signals that move the settings over time[cite: 1]. Modulation does not replace the audio path; it moves the dials along it[cite: 1].
